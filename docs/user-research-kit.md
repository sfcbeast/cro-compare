# CRO Compare: user research kit

Goal: find out, from 5 people who really buy lab services, whether this site helps them find a better option faster than they would have without it, and what is missing.

Do not try to sell or explain the site. Watch what they do.

## 1. Who to ask

People who have ordered an outside service in the last 12 months (sequencing, plasmid verification, RNA-seq, a cell assay):

- PIs and lab managers of small academic labs
- Postdocs and senior grad students who place orders
- Scientists at startups or small biotechs with no procurement team

Aim for 5 people across at least 2 of those groups. Friends of friends are fine; close friends tend to be too kind.

## 2. Message you can send (edit freely)

> Hi [name], I am building a free site that compares prices for lab services (sequencing, plasmid verification, RNA-seq, cell assays) so small labs can find the cheapest option without emailing five providers. Could I borrow 20 minutes to watch you use it on a real order you have placed or are planning? I am not selling anything, and I would like honest criticism more than praise.

## 3. Session script (20 minutes)

**Before (3 min)**
- "What did you last order from outside, and how did you decide who to use?"
- "How long did that take? Did you compare at least two providers?"

**Task (10 min)**: do not explain the site. Say:
- "Imagine you need [their real recent order]. Use this site to decide who to use."
- Stay quiet. If they stall for more than 30 seconds, note where, then give a one-line nudge.

**After (7 min)**
1. "Would you have chosen differently from what you ordered last time?"
2. "What did you not trust, or what would make you double-check?"
3. "What did you look for that was not there?"
4. "Would you come back for your next order? Why or why not?"
5. "If a provider paid to be listed higher, how would that change your trust?"

Do not ask "would you pay?" as a yes/no. Ask: "How do you pay for software or services you use for work today?"

Also note every wrong or outdated price they spot; send each one through the site's "Report a problem" link so it is logged.

## 5. What counts as a good result

After 5 sessions, the idea is working if:

- At least 3 finish the task without help.
- At least 3 either find a cheaper option or confirm their own choice with more confidence.
- At least 3 say they would use it for their next order.

If most people finish the task but nobody would return, the data is too thin or not their service categories: the next work is more providers in the categories they asked for, not new features.

## 6. Ground rules

- Ask permission before taking notes or recording, and do not share names.
- Use their real order, not a made-up one; made-up tasks hide the real problems.
- Write down what they did, not only what they said.
